Statistical problems are cheapest to fix before the data are collected. An underpowered experiment, a design that confounds the effects you want to separate, or a sampling scheme that doesn’t match your analysis can’t be rescued after the fact, however sophisticated the methods. The most valuable time to consult a statistician is at the planning stage. I work with researchers to design experiments that answer their questions efficiently. That includes choosing an appropriate design, determining how many replicates, sites, or seasons are needed, and running power analyses to make sure the study can detect the effects that matter.

Once the data are in, I help you choose methods that fit both the structure of your data and the questions you are asking. Agricultural and environmental data often involve repeated measures, spatial structure, unbalanced designs, non-normal responses, or nested sources of variation. I can advise on which approaches are appropriate, from mixed and nonlinear models to Bayesian methods, and explain the trade-offs so you understand and can defend the choices made. I can also help you interpret the output correctly and present your results in figures, tables, and methods sections that will hold up to peer review.

Because my training is in agronomy as well as statistics, I understand the scientific context of your work: the realities of field and greenhouse research, the conventions of your discipline, and what reviewers and editors in your field expect to see. This means less time explaining your study and more time solving the problem.

Consulting can be a single conversation to settle a specific question or an ongoing collaboration over the life of a project. Consultations are held remotely. In-person work is available if the client covers travel and accommodation costs.
